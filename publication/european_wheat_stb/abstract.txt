Septoria tritici blotch reduces wheat yield, making its response to climate change important for European food production. How changing climate alters disease damage through wheat development remains uncertain across European growing regions. We linked models of wheat development and Septoria tritici blotch across 14,932 wheat-growing grid cells, using three climate models and three emissions pathways. Under SSP5–8.5 in 2071–2100, healthy-area-duration (HAD) loss normalized to reference leaf area increased by 2.66 days relative to 1991–2020. Regional changes ranged from -1.40 days in the Mediterranean to +7.84 days in the Boreal region. Relative HAD loss increased by 8.04 percentage points. Changes in wheat development offset 71% of the weather contribution in a sampled analysis. The canopy–yield relationship implied a yield change of -47.8 kg ha⁻¹ per unit reference leaf area index. These regional contrasts inform monitoring and adaptation trials, with disease-related yield estimates requiring field validation.
