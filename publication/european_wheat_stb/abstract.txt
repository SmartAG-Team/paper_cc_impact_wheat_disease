Septoria tritici blotch threatens European wheat production, but its response to climate change depends on both pathogen and crop development. How changes in wheat development modify disease damage across European growing regions remains poorly quantified. We projected regional canopy damage using linked models of wheat development and Septoria disease, with climate projections from three models under three emissions pathways. Under SSP5–8.5 in 2071–2100, normalized healthy-area-duration loss increased by 2.66 days relative to 1991–2020, with increasing damage over 77.3% of reference wheat area. Regional changes ranged from -1.40 days in the Mediterranean to +7.84 days in the Boreal region. Relative healthy-area-duration loss increased by 8.04 percentage points. Wheat development offset an estimated 71% of the increase in canopy damage attributable to weather. These projections identify crop development as a major control on disease damage and reveal contrasting regional priorities for protecting European wheat production.
