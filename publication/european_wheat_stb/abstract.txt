Septoria tritici blotch threatens European wheat production as climate change alters conditions for disease. How shifts in leaf emergence, flowering and grain filling modify future disease damage remains poorly quantified. We linked wheat phenology and Septoria disease models to project canopy damage across Europe using three climate models and three emissions pathways. Under SSP5–8.5 in 2071–2100, normalized loss of healthy-area duration during grain filling increased by 2.66 days relative to 1991–2020, with greater damage across 77.3% of reference wheat area. Regional changes ranged from -1.40 days in the Mediterranean to +7.84 days in the Boreal region. The fraction of healthy-area duration lost increased by 8.04 percentage points. Shifts in wheat phenology offset an estimated 71% of the increase in canopy damage attributable to weather. These projections show that crop timing strongly modifies climate-driven disease damage, with contrasting regional priorities for protecting European wheat production.
