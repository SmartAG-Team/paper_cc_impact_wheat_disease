Septoria tritici blotch threatens European wheat production as climate change alters conditions for disease. How crop phenology modifies regional disease impacts and the wheat production exposed to them remains unresolved. We combined phenology–disease modelling, field evaluation and fixed wheat-production weights across Europe under three climate models and emissions pathways. Under SSP5–8.5 in 2071–2100, normalized loss of healthy-area duration during grain filling increased by 2.66 days relative to 1991–2020, with all three models agreeing on increasing damage in cells representing 67.1% of baseline wheat production. Continental production exposure was nearly six times that of the Boreal region. Shifts in wheat phenology offset an estimated 71% of the increase in canopy damage attributable to weather. However, disease and yield-response predictions did not consistently outperform benchmarks. These conditional projections identify regional priorities for monitoring and field trials; harvested-yield losses and adaptation benefits require compatible canopy-function and grain-yield observations.
