Climate-driven crop disease threatens the stability of European wheat production. The spatial consequences of changing disease–crop timing for canopy damage remain poorly quantified. We coupled wheat and Septoria simulations across 14,932 land-use grids with fixed winter-wheat calendars, three climate models and three emissions pathways. Under SSP5–8.5 in 2071–2100 relative to 1991–2020, the European healthy-area-duration deficit changed by +2.66 days per reference leaf area. Regional changes ranged from -1.40 in the Mediterranean region to +7.84 days in the Boreal region. Mean canopy damage changed by +8.04 percentage points, with a conditional yield change of -47.8 kg ha⁻¹ per reference leaf area. A diagnostic decomposition attributed a 71% offset of the disease-weather contribution to crop development. Yield consequences remained conditional on canopy–yield conversion, and disease prediction skill remained limited. These regional contrasts support joint evaluation of crop calendars, disease resistance and canopy function for resilient wheat production.
