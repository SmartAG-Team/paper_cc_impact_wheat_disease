Methods

Wheat-growing area and climate data

The European SPAM2020 all-wheat registry contains 14,941 cells with positive harvested wheat area at 0.25° resolution. A fixed GGCMI winter-wheat rainfed calendar is available for 14,932 cells; nine cells have unavailable simulation outcomes. The all-wheat area mask combined with a winter-wheat rainfed calendar defines the crop-management scenario. It is not a census of observed winter-wheat management. Harvested area provides fixed weights for spatial aggregation. Supplementary Fig. S21 shows the study area, field-observation locations and environmental regions. [16, 17]

Daily NEX-GDDP-CMIP6v2 data represent ACCESS-CM2, MPI-ESM1-2-HR and MRI-ESM2-0 under SSP1–2.6, SSP2–4.5 and SSP5–8.5. The harvest periods are 1991–2020, 2031–2060 and 2071–2100. The 810 model–scenario–year combinations cover 12,094,920 calendar-eligible grid-seasons. Monthly adjustment coefficients align climate variables with the reference weather data, with separate historical adjustments for each SSP continuation. Daily series include the preceding calendar year to represent overwintering seasons. Input files, adjustment coefficients and model configurations are identified by checksums. [18]

Disease calculations use daily temperature, relative humidity and rainfall. A synthetic reconstruction of humidity within each day estimates moisture exposure from daily forcing; it does not supply measured leaf wetness. Missing weather and daily mean temperatures above 40 °C produce unavailable outcomes under the model input rules.

Linking wheat development to Septoria disease

A temperature–photoperiod–vernalization model calculates wheat development from sowing to soft dough. Additional ordered developmental thresholds determine upper-leaf appearance and unfolding, and a calibrated flowering threshold defines the start of the grain-filling interval. These crop trajectories determine which leaves are available to infection and the seasonal weather experienced by each leaf. The Septoria module calculates disease establishment, progression and visible damage using weather and these leaf trajectories. Disease damage does not alter the calculated crop-development trajectory. Model equations, parameter sources and observation definitions appear in the supplementary model specification and the 84-entry parameter inventory. [6, 7, 8, 9, 10]

Calibrated parameters remain fixed across climate scenarios. The maximum reference LAI of the upper three leaves is one, divided equally among leaves and scaled by unfolding. Cultivar-specific canopy area and future changes in resistance are not estimated. Soft dough marks season completion. Missing crop calendars, invalid weather and failure to reach the endpoint remain separate unavailable outcomes, rather than zero disease or zero loss.

Symptoms, canopy damage and healthy-area duration

Symptom frequency is the fraction of complete simulated seasons with detectable flag-leaf symptoms before soft dough. First-symptom timing is measured relative to flowering or sowing and is unavailable when symptoms are not detected. Symptomatic grain-fill days count days with detectable flag-leaf symptoms between flowering and soft dough. Symptoms are visible model states following disease development; their dates are distinct from the modelled dates of initial infection.

The disease-damage fraction after symptom detection is treated as the fraction of reference leaf area that has lost function. This assumption connects modelled disease to canopy damage. HAD loss integrates this fraction multiplied by daily reference leaf area across the flag leaf and two leaves below it. The primary interval is flowering to soft dough (GS65–85), with GS31–85 used for sensitivity analysis. Unnormalized HAD has units of green leaf area index multiplied by days (GLAI-days). Normalized HAD loss divides it by maximum reference upper-canopy LAI and has units of days. With reference LAI equal to one, normalization leaves numerical values unchanged. Relative HAD loss is HAD loss divided by reference HAD, expressed as a percentage; it describes average accumulated canopy-function loss, not instantaneous lesion percentage.

Historical–future comparisons and spatial summaries

Each future harvest year is paired with the corresponding position in the 30-year historical period for the same climate model and SSP. This indexing supports comparisons of period means without identifying historical and future years as the same physical event. A pair is included only when both metric values are finite. For grid i, x_i is the sum of valid paired differences divided by 30, and z_i is the number of valid pairs divided by 30. The grid mean change is x_i/z_i. Reference and future means use the same valid pairs. The valid seasons may differ among symptom timing, symptom frequency and canopy metrics.

For domain indicator I_i and fixed harvested area w_i, the mean change is Σw_i I_i x_i / Σw_i I_i z_i. Separate estimates for each climate model give the reported three-model mean and range. EEA 2016 boundaries assign environmental regions from wheat-cell centroids. Country assignment uses the dominant SPAM source-country label of each cell; cross-border cells are not divided among countries. Country estimates apply only to represented wheat cells within the study domain. Source Data includes all eight named environmental regions, outside-region and unassigned cells, and all country groups. [19]

Maps show model means at the native 0.25° grid resolution, without interpolation or filling unavailable cells. Area shares by direction of change use fixed harvested-area weights over cells with complete three-model estimates. Model ranges describe spread among three climate models and are not confidence intervals. All wheat cells are simulated, so full-grid estimates have no spatial-sampling Monte Carlo error. Other uncertainties include climate-model choice, model parameters, observations and the canopy–yield relationship. The twelve countries in Fig. 3 are selected by reference wheat area independently of the projected change.

Estimating the disease-related yield response

The estimated disease-related yield change is −b times the change in HAD loss. The reference coefficient b is 0.018 t ha⁻¹ per GLAI-day, with 0.0141 and 0.0207 used as fixed alternatives from the published upper-three-leaf study. Multiplication by 1,000 converts t ha⁻¹ to kg ha⁻¹. Results are expressed per unit reference upper-canopy LAI because actual canopy area is unspecified. The source relationship began at GS31, whereas the primary climate calculation covers GS65–85; its use in this shorter interval is an unvalidated adaptation. Coefficient alternatives represent cultivar differences, not statistical confidence limits. The calculation estimates a disease-related yield component under these assumptions, without modelling the direct effects of heat, water limitation, CO₂ or other stresses on total grain yield. Yield percentages and national production tonnages require independently validated canopy area and grain yield. [2, 11]

Separating weather and wheat-development effects

The decomposition and fourteen-setting sensitivity analysis use 64 harvested-area-proportional draws at 62 unique cells. Four simulations combine historical or future disease weather with historical or future wheat-development trajectories. Their differences separate weather and wheat-development contributions, with one half of their interaction allocated to each Shapley contribution. Of 5,751 finite historical–future pairs, 20 have an active day outside the accepted temperature range in a recombined trajectory and are excluded, leaving 5,731 pairs. Spatial Monte Carlo standard errors apply to this sample and are separate from full-grid climate estimates.

The development offset is −100 times the development contribution divided by the weather contribution, using identical valid pairs. The ensemble percentage is the ratio of three-model mean contributions. Sensitivity settings vary model structure, symptom detection, reference canopy area and damage-to-function conversion. These calculations assess model assumptions and do not represent trials of a sowing-date or cultivar intervention.

Field evaluation

Disease observations from the two public field archives are pooled after exclusion of calibration records and application of geographic eligibility criteria. The evaluation contains 2,033 assessments from 188 field seasons in 140 coordinate-years and 136 symptom-onset histories from 78 field seasons in 62 coordinate-years. Onset histories concern source-defined leaves 1–3 and have observations bracketing symptom appearance. Original identifiers, units and percentage denominators remain in the evidence files. Source leaf numbers do not establish a common final-leaf rank across archives. [20, 21]

Evaluation weights are equal among coordinate-years, fields within coordinate-years, source leaves within fields and assessments within leaves. Symptom-onset error measures distance outside the two-sided observation interval, with the predefined penalty for missed positive observations. Severity predictions are compared with the calibration-leaf-mean benchmark; timing and detection use the phenology benchmark selected on calibration data. Model and benchmark predictions are compared on identical records. The 95% intervals use 20,000 paired bootstrap resamples of entire coordinate-year groups. They describe uncertainty in these fixed predictions on reused retrospective observations and exclude parameter-selection uncertainty. Duplicate coordinate-year identities are grouped across archives.

Wheat development is evaluated on 40,878 matched events from German stations held out of calibration, at BBCH 10, 31, 51 and 85. Added leaf stages use pooled field observation intervals, with calibration observations reported separately. Evaluation of the canopy–yield relationship uses eight published treatment-mean contrasts across five genetic backgrounds, including STB, yellow rust and mixed disease. Leaving each background out of fitting gives backgrounds equal evaluation weight. Severity-based yield models use their specified held-out trial or year comparisons and corresponding training-mean benchmarks. [11]

Reproducibility

Annual outputs record input-weather identities, model configurations, grid-cell order, numerical checks and output hashes. Aggregation records source hashes, counts of valid pairs and spatial coverage for each metric. Independent calculations check pooled field scores and selected full-grid regional means. Source Data contains figure and table values. The software archive regenerates documents from completed numerical evidence; a climate-driven model rerun additionally requires the external raw weather products.
