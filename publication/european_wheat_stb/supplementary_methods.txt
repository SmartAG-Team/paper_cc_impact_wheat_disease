Supplementary Methods

Crop, disease and yield relationships

Daily climate data drive wheat-phenology and Septoria-disease calculations. Wheat phenology determines leaf appearance, unfolding and the flowering-to-soft-dough interval. The disease model predicts damage on those leaves; its damage does not feed back into crop-development rates. After symptom detection, damage is mapped to an assumed loss of leaf function. Integration gives HAD loss, and a published coefficient converts HAD loss to a conditional disease-related yield estimate. This final calculation is separate from a complete crop-yield model.

The reference upper-three-leaf canopy has maximum LAI of one. Disease-induced HAD loss integrates the assumed loss of functional green leaf area over time, with units of GLAI-days. Division by maximum reference LAI gives normalized HAD loss, expressed as equivalent days of reference canopy function lost. Numerical values are identical in the unit-LAI scenario. Relative HAD loss is the ratio of HAD loss to reference HAD and describes loss of assumed canopy function over the integration interval. Measured leaf-lesion percentage has a different denominator and temporal meaning.

Full-grid climate estimates

The experiment includes all 14,941 wheat land-use cells, with eligible calendars for 14,932 cells. Invalid seasons have unavailable outcomes. Symptom timing requires detection, while symptom frequency includes complete seasons without symptoms. Each metric has its own valid historical–future pairs. Corresponding positions in the two 30-year periods are paired within a climate model and SSP.

Spatial means divide the harvested-area-weighted sum of paired changes by the harvested-area-weighted number of valid pairs. Historical and future means use these same pairs. Climate-model ranges span three independently aggregated estimates. The full-grid calculation has no spatial sampling error. Environmental regions follow EEA centroid assignment and countries follow dominant SPAM source-country labels; outside-region and unassigned cells remain explicit.

Field observations and prediction scores

Pooled disease evaluation excludes calibration records and combines geographically eligible records from both archives. Source identifiers and assessment definitions remain in the evidence files. Calibration and evaluation have no shared field identifiers. Coordinate-year identities define geographic bootstrap groups, with overlapping identities treated as the same group.

An assessment weight is the reciprocal of the number of coordinate-years multiplied by the reciprocals of fields, source leaves and observations within successive groups. RMSE is the square root of the weighted mean squared error. Twenty thousand paired coordinate-year bootstrap resamples, with seed 20261008, give 95% percentile intervals. Every resample retains both predictions and all field, leaf and date records in a sampled group. The intervals exclude parameter-selection uncertainty and verification of independent biological-site identities.

Symptom-onset comparisons use observed intervals bracketing first symptoms and specified penalties for missed positive observations. Crop-stage evaluation also uses two-sided observation intervals; plotted interval centres are display locations, not exact observed event dates. Calibration profiles and nested model selection are separate from pooled evaluation. German station observations evaluate the established phenology formulation; field support for added upper-leaf stages and soft dough remains limited.

Canopy-based yield estimates

Published protected and unprotected treatment means retain their leaf coverage, growth-stage interval, cultivar and disease exposure. The eight contrasts across five backgrounds include two with STB alone, two with yellow rust and four with mixed diseases. Absolute and relative yield scores describe aggregate treatment means when each genetic background is excluded from fitting. Raw plot variation and covariance are unavailable.

The grid yield estimate is a linear conversion of HAD loss under the assumed upper-three-leaf canopy. Fixed coefficients and alternative GS31–85 and GS65–85 intervals assess dependence on these assumptions. The primary interval is shorter than the source coefficient interval, so the conversion requires field validation. Positive projected yield changes denote a decrease in the estimated disease-loss component; they do not establish a gain in total yield under future climate.

Severity-based field yield comparisons

The consolidated Tunisia/Nordic inventory contains 423 dated leaf or treatment assessments sharing 272 yield keys. Exclusion of a known control alias and post-harvest assessments leaves 412 assessments and 263 outcomes. These inventory counts do not denote independent environments. The Nordic predictive population contains 62 treated–untreated contrasts in five trials; subsets with known assessment stage and an earlier matched assessment contain 51 and 40 contrasts, respectively. Missing treated-plot stem-extension assessments remain unavailable. Growth-stage interactions use recorded ordinal GS, rather than elapsed time. The fixed stage-regression basis is severity reduction multiplied by (GS − 75)/10. The secondary nonnegative basis uses severity reduction multiplied by (85 − GS)/10. Neither basis represents observed infection timing.

The Tunisian plot table contains incidence-weighted lesion area on flag and immediately lower leaves. Flag leaves were assessed at GS75 in both years; lower leaves were assessed at GS61 in 2018 and GS73 in 2019. The leaf-stage and year differences prevent separate identification of leaf-specific physiological coefficients. No same-leaf AUDPC or HAD is reconstructed. Cultivar proportions define composition predictors and shared mixture identities; treatment numbers are year-specific. Two-way whole-year transfer evaluates absolute yield on 82 outcomes. The 40 source-matched reference contrasts retain one negative response; within-year validation excludes entire mixture compositions or source replicate groups. These comparisons remain within one site-year and do not establish external environmental validity.

German source harmonization preserves the original site-table precision and follows the documented source treatment coding, while excluding drought-suffixed WF references from unsuffixed NF/WF pairs. One nonnumeric severity token remains unavailable. Treatment means retain yield and severity replicate counts separately; missing protected severity is not imputed. The primary endpoint population contains 3,264 comparisons across 16 site-years and five locations. The subset with observed severity on both management groups contains 954 comparisons across seven site-years and four locations. Common-population benchmarks accompany each comparison. The primary and temporal comparisons give site-years equal weight; geographic transfer gives locations equal weight. Site-specific rating comparability and incomplete assessment timing remain limitations.

The complete source inventory includes raw field observations, regional survey aggregates, published model-adjusted means, fitted curves and model inputs. Fitted response grids, duplicate archive copies and published parameter tables do not increase observational sample size. Older Nordic/Baltic yield and weather-derived recommendation tables do not provide observed severity–yield pairs. New data-source identities, eligibility, fixed protocols, held-out predictions and independent numerical checks are retained with the current source data.

Weather and wheat-phenology contributions

The separate decomposition uses four historical–future combinations of disease weather and wheat-phenology trajectories in the 64-draw sample. Twenty pairs exceeding the accepted temperature range in a recombined trajectory are excluded. The two Shapley contributions share the interaction equally and sum to the net change on the same valid pairs. Phenology offsets use the identical pair set in numerator and denominator. The three-model ensemble offset is a ratio of mean contributions.

The main maps and regional estimates describe the completed full-grid experiment. Decomposition and structural sensitivity describe the separate sampled analysis. Field figures describe prediction errors on retrospective observations, and published yield figures describe treatment-mean contrasts. These datasets support different quantities and do not define a single overall model-accuracy percentage.
