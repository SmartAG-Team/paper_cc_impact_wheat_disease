A paired two-factor comparison separated the disease-weather forcing bundle from the complete modeled host-development trajectory under SSP585. The four combinations comprised reference weather with reference host development, future weather with reference host development, reference weather with future host development, and future weather with future host development. All three GCMs and sixty-four registered spatial draws were retained for thirty historical–future year pairs per GCM. The diagonal combinations are identical to the verified current-kernel baseline; the two hybrid combinations retain the selected source and disease parameters. Disease-weather forcing comprises daily mean and maximum temperature, humidity and precipitation entering the existing exposure and disease-progression calculations. Host development comprises leaf availability, unfolding area, tissue renewal, stage crossings and the crop-window mask. The selected host trajectory retains its own GS85 endpoint and GS65–85 or GS31–85 HAD window.

The alignment coordinate is elapsed calendar days since each crop-calendar sowing date. Consecutive cached daily climate-forcing values are consumed once in order, without interpolation, repeated leap-day values or active-day padding. The 2020–2100 pair retains the reference leap day through elapsed-day alignment, producing a one-day month/day offset after 29 February. Every active target crop day has an available source-forcing value. Each outcome uses the same draw, GCM and relative-year pairs with finite values in all four combinations. With outputs denoted Y00, Y10, Y01 and Y11 for reference/reference, future-weather/reference-host, reference-weather/future-host and future/future, the interaction is I = Y11 − Y10 − Y01 + Y00. The order-invariant disease-weather contribution is (Y10 − Y00) + I/2, and the host-development contribution is (Y01 − Y00) + I/2. Their sum equals the total change Y11 − Y00. The interaction is reported separately and is already allocated equally within the two Shapley contributions.

The host-development contribution was −6.332744 days, with a GCM range of −7.738246 to −5.237997 and a spatial Monte Carlo standard error of 0.376197. Their sum was the baseline net increase of 2.616640 days. The interaction was −2.761504 days, with a GCM range of −3.088273 to −2.369731 and a spatial Monte Carlo standard error of 0.298503. At the reference host trajectory, changing the weather alone increased the proxy by 10.330136 days; at reference weather, changing the host alone decreased it by 4.951992 days. Complete four-combination paired coverage was 99.9719–100%, representing 1,913–1,920 draw-year pairs per GCM.

The host component opposed the disease-weather contribution through the complete shifted developmental trajectory and crop window. Modeled anthesis advanced by a mean of 26.769245 days after sowing, and GS85 advanced by 26.644328 days. Grain-fill duration changed by only +0.124918 days, with a GCM range of −0.548785 to +1.053835 days. The HAD offset therefore cannot be attributed simply to shorter grain filling. Earlier stage progression changes the elapsed-season timing of leaf availability, tissue renewal and the anthesis-to-GS85 window under each retained weather sequence.

Its denominator differs from the baseline-only symptom comparison because all four combinations require detected events in both host–weather configurations.

These signed contributions describe dependence within the current mathematical model. The paired weather and host components are coupled in physical crop systems, and their hybrid combinations have no independent physiological validation. Mean disease-weather temperatures exceeded 40 °C on 42 of 3,158,738 active hybrid forcing days; the retained disease-progression kernel uses its existing linear nonnegative-temperature clock on these days, while host-development validity remains tied to the host's own forcing. The underlying disease model, source routes, damage-to-functional-area conversion and yield-transfer relation remain unvalidated for quantitative crop-production attribution. The decomposition identifies modeled infection-timing and canopy-proxy pathways; it does not identify physical causal effects or actual yield losses.
