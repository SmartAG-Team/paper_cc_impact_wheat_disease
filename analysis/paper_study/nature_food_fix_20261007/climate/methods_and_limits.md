The robustness ensemble comprised fourteen registered scenarios under SSP585 for 1991–2020 and 2071–2100, using ACCESS-CM2, MPI-ESM1-2-HR and MRI-ESM2-0 and the original sixty-four spatial draws. The spatial design retained four independent draws with replacement in each of sixteen strata, including repeated cells with distinct draw identifiers. Each historical draw-year was paired with the same draw and GCM eighty years later. No additional random draws, bootstrap resampling or validation-based parameter selection entered the ensemble. The seven disease–host trajectories comprised the frozen baseline, a runner-up tied on the primary inner-calibration objective, zero initial local source, effective rank spacings of eighty and one hundred sixty donor development units, and early and late stage profiles. The runner-up lost the original prespecified balanced-error tie-break and did not replace the selected fit. The stage alternatives were strictly ordered paths at the earliest or latest admissible C37 threshold, with joint stage-calibration loss bounded by one day above the original minimum. These paths are declared structural stress scenarios and have no calibrated probability distribution or confidence-interval interpretation.

First affected-tissue and symptom crossings used detection conventions of 0.0001, 0.001 and 0.01. Alternative conventions were extracted from the same daily trajectory. The upper-three-leaf healthy-area-duration loss proxy equalled the daily sum of modeled unfolding area divided by three, multiplied by modeled damage after the first modeled symptom crossing. Reference-canopy sensitivities multiplied this area by a factor that remained one before GS65 and declined linearly in elapsed calendar days to 0.5 or zero at GS85. Functional-conversion sensitivities multiplied symptom-gated modeled damage by 0.5 or 0.75; a combined scenario used decline to zero and conversion of 0.5. These reference and conversion factors are explicit transfer assumptions, with no observational calibration. Reference decline altered the HAD weighting while retaining the integrated disease trajectory. Both GS65–85 and GS31–85 windows were retained. Fixed yield-transfer coefficients of 0.0141, 0.018 and 0.0207 expressed the diagnostic in conditional tonnes per hectare per unit nominal upper-three reference LAI.

Area-time ratio estimates used the original harvested-area weights, with missing outcomes excluded from both numerator and denominator. Within-scenario changes used historical–future pairs with finite outcomes. Cross-scenario comparisons additionally used the intersection of these pairs across all fourteen scenarios for each GCM and metric. Event-timing comparisons were conditional on detection in both periods. Spatial Monte Carlo standard errors used ratio-residual linearization with four independent draws per stratum. GCM-mean uncertainty retained the covariance induced by shared spatial draws by averaging GCM influence vectors before variance estimation. The three-GCM range describes climate-model spread, and the spatial Monte Carlo standard error describes spatial sampling error; neither represents full parameter or predictive uncertainty.

The retrospective disease model has poor discrimination in stricter validation, and its calibration does not identify disease magnitude or source routes. Fixed positive source assumptions tend to saturate modeled occurrence. The nominal reference canopy, natural senescence, disease-to-functional-area conversion and yield-transfer relationship remain unvalidated for the modeled population. A winter-wheat/rainfed calendar scenario applies to an all-wheat area registry. The ensemble quantifies conditional modeled event timing and HAD proxy changes. European disease-attributable production losses, adaptation effectiveness, economic returns and policy benefits are not identified by these calculations. Cached baseline results under SSP126 and SSP245 provide scenario context; the registered structural ensemble applies to SSP585.
