Infection occurrence, timing and upper-leaf yield relevance

The primary prediction targets are effective infection events, first visible symptoms, and infection of the final three leaves during grain filling. Numerical infection percentages are secondary descriptors and do not determine event-model calibration. The event model uses zero versus positive assessment labels and censored symptom-detection brackets. Weather-driven infection dates remain model-inferred, conditional on inoculum presence. Seasonal occurrence probabilities are not calibrated by the predominately disease-positive archive.

Host availability follows the transferred crop-development clock and independent leaf emergence/expansion assumptions. Infection requires cumulative temperature-weighted humid/rain exposure during a wet spell; an explicitly declared dry gap resets inadequate dose. A positive effective dose threshold prevents dry days alone from producing an infection event. A thermal delay separates effective infection from visible symptoms. The model generates distinct dates for ranks F1, F2 and F3 and does not initialize disease from observations.

The primary timing score is distance outside a two-sided assessment bracket, with explicit missing-positive penalties and no midpoint imputation. Complete location histories and source units share folds. Independently selected phenology-only timing is the principal benchmark. Assessment-date sensitivity and specificity measure detection at the observed dates; they do not establish specificity for infection-free whole seasons. A first-onset interpretation of the bracket requires no earlier transient crossing followed by a return to zero.

Yield relevance depends on infection and functional green-area loss during the crop's source-active period. A stage-only calibrated BBCH65 development threshold supplies an anthesis proxy, with BBCH85 as the soft-dough boundary of the main truncated grain-fill window. Unreached or incomplete crop windows remain missing. Infection overlap with grain filling is an exposure descriptor, not a yield-loss fraction.

The quantitative yield operator integrates functional green-area deficit in the top three leaves: DeltaHAD3 = integral(sum_i=1..3 LAI_reference_i(t) * functional_loss_i(t)) dt. Literature transfer uses DeltaY = b * DeltaHAD3, with b in tonnes per hectare per GLAI-day. The Parker et al. (2004) random-effects centre is 0.0180 and reported cultivar slope range 0.0141–0.0207. The range is a cultivar sensitivity, not a global confidence interval. Actual layer areas determine contributions; no universal fixed F1/F2/F3 weights are assumed.

Reference leaf area, functional loss fractions and, for relative yield loss, an independent reference yield are explicit inputs. Binary infection indicators are not substituted for complete functional loss. An infection score is not silently converted to green leaf area. Without those canopy inputs, the runtime supplies infection timing and grain-fill exposure while leaving numerical yield loss undefined. Literature coefficients are conditional transfers without local matched-yield validation. The main anthesis-to-BBCH85 window differs from the published whole observed upper-leaf HAD window; equivalence requires zero disease-induced loss outside the chosen interval.

Retrospective nested evaluation, all-data event refitting, stage-only anthesis calibration, conditional yield scenarios and future-climate simulations retain separate evidence roles. Previously examined outcomes are development evidence. Original severity and climate archives retain their original model identities.
