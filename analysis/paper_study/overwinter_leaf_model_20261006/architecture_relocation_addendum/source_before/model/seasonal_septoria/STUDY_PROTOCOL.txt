Seasonal crop-protection prediction and European climate assessment

Estimands
The seasonal predictor is initialized with susceptible wheat at crop establishment. Disease observations are evaluation targets and cannot initialize this predictor. The earlier assessment-initialized model remains a separate within-season forecasting benchmark. Primary and secondary labels denote modelled infection routes; field severity measurements do not identify the route of individual lesions.

Observed disease occurrence is detection within a documented assessment window. An all-zero record ending before the crop's final assessment is right-censored for subsequent detection. Occurrence and severity are indexed by source organ and measurement definition. Positive-selected leaves do not estimate population disease occurrence. Final severity is the last numeric untreated assessment, with its date and organ retained; it is not automatically harvest severity.

First visible disease is interval-censored between the last negative and first positive assessment of the same observation unit. A first assessment already positive is left-censored. The seasonal model also supplies an inferred primary infection date, which has no direct field reference in the currently acquired trials. Delta is defined provisionally as the prediction error for first visible disease; its sign is predicted date minus reference date. An interval-compatible error is zero inside an observed onset interval and the signed distance to the nearest boundary outside it. Exact-date MAE requires an exact observed date and cannot be calculated by silently substituting interval midpoints.

Calibration and validation
The partition unit is an independent field-season, identified by dataset, source site and season. Cultivars, plots, leaves and assessment dates from the same field-season share a partition. Source field identity takes precedence over rounded coordinates. Coordinate clusters additionally remain grouped for spatial validation. Calibration data determine parameters, model structure choices, weather-response variants, probability thresholds and observation scales. Validation data are used once per declared frozen model. New revisions constitute a new development cycle and cannot turn a reused validation set into an untouched test set.

The historical BASF and French data have been examined during earlier model development. Newly declared partitions in these data are development validation, with this prior exposure disclosed. An independently acquired source or future untouched field season is required for a final external test. Chronological validation reserves the most recent sufficiently populated year. Spatial validation reserves whole source locations. The complete membership table and data hashes precede numerical calibration.

Severity evaluation includes MAE, RMSE, signed bias and prediction-interval coverage, using percentage points. Calibration and primary severity scores give equal total weight to each rounded-coordinate-year group, equal weight to source field-seasons within that group, equal weight to leaf series within each field-season, and equal weight to dates within each series. Final-assessment scores omit the date level. Literal equal-field-season scores are a supplementary estimator. Pycnidial coverage, necrosis and source infection percentages retain distinct observation operators. Presence evaluation includes prevalence, sensitivity, specificity, false-warning rate, balanced accuracy and, for calibrated probabilities, Brier score and calibration. A one-class validation set cannot establish specificity, discrimination or balanced accuracy. Onset evaluation includes the proportion compatible with censoring bounds and signed distance outside those bounds; counts and censoring types accompany every score.

Phenology and host availability
The copied temperature-photoperiod-vernalization model supplies crop timing. Observed sowing dates take precedence over crop-calendar priors. Calendar-derived sowing is identified as an assumption and propagates through uncertainty calculations. Winter and spring crops require separate phenology inputs. The source model's stage85 denotes soft dough. Leaf-cohort emergence interpolated between supported phenology events is an explicit structural approximation. New leaf area dilutes existing infected fractions; inactive cohorts cannot acquire or export infections. Disease starts from zero at sowing and no field disease score is assimilated.

Weather
Field assessment simulations use the archived ERA5-derived UTC daily records. Regional baseline comprises harvest seasons1991–2020, with antecedent1990 forcing for autumn-sown wheat. ERA5 daily mean relative humidity is calculated from hourly temperature and dewpoint pairs before daily averaging. Daily mean relative humidity is not the number of humid hours and is not measured leaf wetness. A daily weather-response model receives the same variable definitions in baseline and climate scenarios.

Climate projections
Future harvest periods are2031–2060 and2071–2100 under SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, with antecedent2030 and2070 where needed. The main ensemble consists of ACCESS-CM2, MPI-ESM1-2-HR and MRI-ESM2-0, conditional on verified complete variables and years. Native NASA NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 data support these scenarios; the Earth Engine collection currently supports SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5. Historical model data end2014, so1991–2020 alignment retains scenario-specific2015–2020 continuation. Individual model, scenario, calendar, realization and version remain in the forcing registry.

Monthly historical-period bias adjustment aligns daily mean temperature means, precipitation means and relative-humidity mean log odds with ERA5 while retaining original forcing. A common temperature offset applies to daily mean and maximum temperature. The adjustment uses historical forcing only and preserves additive temperature changes, relative precipitation changes and relative-humidity changes in log-odds coordinates. It does not adjust wet-day frequency, distribution tails or the full joint distribution. Unadjusted sensitivity accompanies adjusted projections. Changes are calculated relative to each model's aligned1991–2020 baseline. Crop calendars and crop-area distributions are held at their stated reference unless an explicit adaptation scenario is supplied. This isolates climatic exposure under those agricultural assumptions rather than predicting future land allocation.

Spatial support and aggregation
The main reporting grid is0.25degrees, with explicit source projection and resampling metadata. Wheat-specific SPAM physical and harvested areas remain distinct. Physical hectares weight cultivated-land exposure; harvested hectares weight crop-season exposure. Wheat area cannot be duplicated across winter and spring calendar variants. Unsupported season allocations remain unallocated or are reported as separate full-area calendar sensitivities. Geographic Europe includes UK and non-EU wheat regions, with boundary assumptions for Russia and Turkey specified in the spatial registry. Missing source data are not zero hectares.

Regional outputs comprise wheat-area-weighted disease detection frequency, onset distribution, untreated final-severity distribution and differences between future and baseline periods. Annual sequences, climate-model variation, field-model parameter uncertainty, sowing uncertainty and observation-scale uncertainty remain separately identifiable. Geography outside the validated crop and climate range is labelled extrapolation. Disease severity alone does not identify fungicide response, economic loss or an optimal spray programme.

Readiness
Publication conclusions require completed climate-period coverage, source-compatible endpoints, declared validation membership, frozen fits and numerical results reproducible from archived inputs. Poor validation performance is a result and cannot be replaced by selectively favourable models, omitted negative results or unsourced regional maps.
