Wheat area was represented by the wheat-specific WHEA layers in IFPRI SPAM2020 Version 2.0 Release 2, reference year 2020, Harvard Dataverse Version 6 released on 5 May 2026. SPAM estimates crop distributions from agricultural statistics and spatial covariates. The native layers have a 5-arcminute grid in EPSG:4326. Physical area and annual harvested area are separate hectare quantities; irrigated and rainfed layers are retained separately. The wheat layers do not distinguish winter/spring wheat, common/durum wheat or cultivars. Annual harvested hectares and occupied physical hectares have different interpretations and remain separate weighting options. [IFPRI SPAM2020 dataset](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/SWPENT), [MAPSPAM Data Center](https://www.mapspam.info/data/).

The operational geographical domain includes countries classified as European by Natural Earth, with the United Kingdom, Ukraine, Belarus and other non-EU countries included. SPAM administrative ownership, represented by ADM0_NAME and the provider's FIPS0 identifier, determines ordinary-country membership. FIPS0 is not an ISO country code. A bounding envelope of 35°W–75°E and 30–90°N excludes overseas possessions from this domain. European microstates are eligible where source statistics exist. No 72°N cutoff is imposed. Source ownership retains ordinary-country crop hectares at coasts without applying a country-polygon centroid filter. This differs from the supplementary map geometry, whose coastline follows the coarser Natural Earth polygons.

The European portions of Russia, Turkey, Kazakhstan, Georgia and Azerbaijan are defined by overlap with the Natural Earth 1:50-million physical EUROPE polygon, version 5.0.0. That polygon is a cartographic approximation and does not establish a surveyed continental divide. The provider identifies uncertainty in physical-region boundaries. Cyprus is excluded from this primary geographical convention; its 11,696.60 source harvested hectares constitute a separately identifiable political-European extension. Ordinary European islands are included through source country ownership rather than through the physical EUROPE polygon alone. Source administrative labels and the map geometry represent their respective provider conventions. [Natural Earth physical-region documentation](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-physical-vectors/50m-physical-labels/).

For native pixel j in a transcontinental country, the retained fraction f_j is the WGS84 ellipsoidal area of the intersection between its pixel footprint and the declared European polygon divided by the ellipsoidal area of the native footprint, bounded between zero and one. Ordinary-country pixels have f_j=1. This fractional allocation assumes crop hectares are distributed uniformly within each native boundary pixel. The included native source hectares are conserved under that assumption; the fractional hectares are not field-level observations.

The target grid has transform [0.25, 0, −180, 0, −0.25, 90], EPSG:4326, with zero-based rows originating at the north edge and columns at the west edge. The native transform is [1/12, 0, −180, 0, −1/12, 90]. Each target cell contains exactly 3×3 aligned native footprints. Extensive source hectares are summed as A_c=Σ(j∈c) f_j A_j. Pixel identities derive from the source's verified integer grid_code, rather than from rounding coordinates to target centres. Target cell centres are (−179.875+0.25×column, 89.875−0.25×row), with identifiers g025_r{row:03d}_c{column:04d}. Grid-cell areas are calculated on the WGS84 ellipsoid. The target geometry is compatible with the declared ERA5/NEX-GDDP 0.25° sampling grid; climate products with different native transforms require explicit spatial remapping.

The registry contains 14,941 positive-wheat cells in 41 source administrative countries, spanning target-cell edges 9.5°W–59.5°E and 34.75–67.75°N. Included harvested area is 54,405,001.122626 ha, including 417,703.201203 irrigated ha and 53,987,299.257057 rainfed ha. Included physical area is 53,769,454.322135 ha. The six original source layers are conserved individually, with the largest total aggregation difference below 1.5×10⁻⁸ ha. The sum of irrigated and rainfed harvested area exceeds the source total by 1.335635 ha after geographical weighting; the largest native source discrepancy is 1.8 ha. These source discrepancies are preserved without reconciling the layers. No included source physical-wheat area exceeds its ellipsoidal native-pixel area.

Native total-area nodata and valid zero hectares remain distinct. Technology-specific rasters sometimes encode an absent production system as nodata, while CSV counterparts contain zero or omit the technology row. Structural zero is assigned only where the retained total area equals the available complementary production-system area; all such source identity checks have zero residual. Original validity flags remain in the source-pixel table. Target-cell valid, zero-wheat and nodata counts describe all nine total-area source footprints, including footprints outside the selected country's area allocation. A centre-membership alternative for transcontinental native pixels yields 9,827.74 additional harvested hectares relative to footprint overlap, equivalent to 0.0181% of the primary total. This quantifies raster-boundary allocation sensitivity; it does not quantify uncertainty in the cartographic continental divide.

Planting and maturity timing comes from GGCMI Phase 3 crop calendars v1.01, using separate winter/spring wheat and rainfed/irrigated files. Native calendar resolution is 0.5°. A target cell inherits the values of its containing native calendar cell, without interpolating day-of-year values. Fields include planting day, maturity day, growing-season length and provider source index. Calendar timing is static and represents provider calendar information rather than observed sowing or harvest dates for an individual field-year. The terminal field is maturity; no observed harvest date is supplied. Valid calendars cover 14,932 target cells in each scenario. The nine missing target cells contain 3,849.55 harvested hectares, 0.0071% of included all-wheat area, and retain missing flags. [GGCMI Phase 3 calendar repository](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.5062513).

SPAM does not allocate hectares between winter and spring wheat. GGCMI calendar availability does not establish the current planted area of either wheat type. MIRCA-OS v2 provides 2020 Wheat1/Wheat2 subcrop records, harvested area and planting/maturity months, preserved as supplemental provider-season evidence. The MIRCA-OS methodology describes seasonal allocations using existing FAO irrigated calendars and MIRCA2000 rainfed calendars where season-specific statistics are unavailable. These seasonal allocations are not universally observed 2020 cultivar-type shares. The acquired 2020 rainfed Russia records assign all 28,803,013 ha to Wheat1 with September planting and August maturity, while Wheat2 has zero area. Source-season labels alone therefore do not establish a verified matching winter/spring allocation for the SPAM grid. The principal area registry remains unallocated all wheat, and the four calendar scenarios contain no repeated hectares. Scenario outputs cannot be summed as independent area partitions. [MIRCA-OS v2 provider repository](https://www.hydroshare.org/resource/e4582ca0042148338bb5e0148b749ed6/), [MIRCA-OS methodology, Scientific Data](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-04313-w).

Point calendars contain 56 BASF sites and 78 Corteva locations, sampled from containing 0.5° cells using source coordinates only. The table retains native-cell coordinates, source indices, scenario type, source-file hashes, missing flags and ellipsoidal distance to the cell centre, with a maximum distance of 33.01 km. Two rounded Corteva coastal coordinates have missing calendars in all four scenarios. No nearest-land substitution is applied. These planting days are scenario inputs and do not replace unknown observed trial sowing dates. The point lookup uses no disease outcomes.

The 2020 area distribution and static calendars provide fixed land-use and calendar scenarios for climate comparisons. They do not represent historical yearly wheat acreage, future land-use change, cultivar adaptation, regional disease-model validation or treatment response. Independent verification reopens acquired source files, checks archive and extracted-file hashes, reconstructs spatial membership, checks all area sums, and verifies gridded and point calendar values. Source acquisition receipts preserve provider URLs, byte counts, checksums, licenses and metadata discrepancies.
