Regional meteorological layers derive from the [ERA5 monthly aggregate](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/ECMWF_ERA5_MONTHLY) and [ERA5 hourly](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/ECMWF_ERA5_HOURLY) collections in Google Earth Engine. Read access was verified on 4 October 2026 using project ee-gangzhaomodel and the existing client-library credentials. The monthly collection contains 498 images and ends in June 2020. Monthly temperature values are means and total precipitation values are sums, including liquid and frozen precipitation. Monthly averages cannot identify hourly rain–humidity conjunctions or infection timing.

The Europe-region domain spans 25°W–65°E and 34–72°N. The geographic mask consists of [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/) country polygons classified as Europe, together with the Cyprus polygon; Russian territory is additionally restricted to longitude ≤60°E. The United Kingdom, Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, Ukraine and other non-EU countries are included. Turkey, North Africa and Arctic islands north of 72°N are excluded. The Cyprus mask follows the Natural Earth Cyprus feature; the separately represented Northern Cyprus feature is excluded. This domain represents a documented European study region rather than the entire geophysical continent. Boundaries at 1:110m omit some small states and islands. Grid cells are selected by their centres within the country polygons; the mask covers all land types and does not identify wheat fields.

The fixed intervals for observation years 2017, 2018 and 2019 extend from 1 September in the preceding year to 30 September in the observation year, inclusive. Each interval comprises 13 complete months and 395 days. Mean 2 m temperature is the calendar-day-weighted mean of monthly temperatures, converted from Kelvin to degrees Celsius. Total precipitation is the sum of monthly totals, converted from metres to millimetres. The six-band GeoTIFF and accompanying table retain the monthly collection's native 0.25° registration: EPSG:4326 with origin (−180°, 90°). The 22,140 selected grid centres represent approximately 9.779 million km² of complete grid-cell area; this quantity is neither exact land area nor wheat area.

The hourly collection uses a separate native 0.25° registration with origin (−180.125°, 90.125°), giving a 0.125° centre offset relative to the monthly product. Monthly and hourly identifiers and masks remain separate. The hourly extraction supports windows of 1–31 completed UTC days and an exclusive end date. Mean temperature, wind speed and derived relative humidity are averaged; precipitation, liquid rain, shortwave energy and threshold-exposure hours are accumulated. Relative humidity uses the Magnus approximation with coefficients 17.625 and 243.04°C. Liquid rain is the nonnegative difference between total precipitation and snowfall water equivalent. Accumulated fields retain the source's preceding-hour convention.

The unmodified hourly-download raster contains 3,728 grid-cell precipitation aggregates marginally below zero, with a minimum of −4.657×10⁻⁶ mm. These magnitudes are physically nonmeaningful numerical artifacts; their encoding or conversion origin is unresolved. Prepared raster and table summaries replace these negative totals with zero, while the original download remains unchanged. Correction counts, magnitudes and checksums are retained in the retrieval metadata. Precipitation below −0.001 mm exceeds the accepted numerical tolerance and stops preparation.

A continental hourly extraction for 1 May 2019 contains 24 source images and 22,198 selected hourly-grid centres. Comparison with the BASF point archive at all 32 available ERA5 grid cells for that date gives maximum absolute differences of 0.035°C for mean temperature and 0.428 mm for daily precipitation. Native humidity and rain values retain greater precision than the rounded Open-Meteo outputs; threshold counts differ by up to two high-humidity hours and four rain hours. This one-day comparison establishes a material input-precision difference, rather than equivalence of model predictors. Compatibility of feature definitions, precision and temporal alignment requires validation before fitted models are transferred between providers.

The [ESA WorldCereal 2021 wintercereals product](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/ESA_WorldCereal_2021_MODELS_v100) is accessible in Earth Engine, with 106 images globally and 16 intersecting the study rectangle. It classifies wheat, barley and rye together and cannot identify winter bread wheat specifically. [IFPRI SPAM2020](https://mapspam.info/) provides modelled all-wheat area as a potential coarse host-area proxy; its original Dataverse API returned HTTP 403, and the advertised derivative raster endpoints returned HTTP 404 during access verification. No wheat-area raster was obtained. A winter-bread-wheat disease forecast additionally requires an appropriate host distribution, planting and phenology information, compatible forcing and demonstrated spatial model performance. The supplied regional artifacts represent weather exposure context and forcing summaries; no Septoria disease model is applied. They contain modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information supplied through Google Earth Engine.
