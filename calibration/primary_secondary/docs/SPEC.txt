Weather-driven primary and secondary Septoria model for wheat crop protection

Scientific target
The applied target is field-level Septoria forecasting on yield-forming wheat leaves, with useful lead time for scouting and protection decisions. Primary establishment, latent development, symptom appearance, sporulation and rain-driven secondary transmission are mathematical components of that forecast. Calibration and validation preserve independent experimental units, source measurement definitions and chronology. Predictive adequacy determines forecasting value; source identifiability determines the strength of quantitative process-attribution claims. Supplementary controlled data constrain model assumptions rather than define the main research outcome. Journal acceptance cannot be determined by a numerical error threshold alone.

State and forcing contract
Each leaf rank has susceptible tissue and three infection-origin labels: unresolved initial infection, externally acquired infection and within-canopy secondary infection. Each origin contains three serial latent stages, visible nonsporulating tissue, infectious tissue and removed tissue. Removed infected tissue retains its recorded damage and historical pycnidial coverage; it contributes no new inoculum. All fractions are nonnegative and sum to one per leaf. All transitions use pre-step states and exponential bounded flows. The numerical step is0.25day; convergence at0.125day is independently checked.

The external hazard is alpha times a temperature-humidity establishment response. The secondary hazard is beta times that response, a rain-splash response and infectious tissue in a normalized leaf-rank transport kernel. Neither alpha nor the modeled external-origin fraction is a measured ascospore supply. Humidity exposure from2m ERA5 is an atmospheric proxy rather than measured leaf wetness. The initial implementation fixes the establishment Gaussian optimum18degC and width8degC, humidity scale24h, splash scale2mm and rank-distance scale1; these are explicit structural assumptions with later process-data calibration required. Thermal development initially scales with positive daily mean temperature relative to18degC. Latent mean and nonsporulating duration are sensitivity inputs, not inferred thermal functions.

Conditional field evaluation
The existing91same-rank BASF sequences remain the initial development cohort. Each forecast episode starts at its target leaf's first assessment. Canopy initialization uses only rank assessments available on or before that episode's start; later disease observations cannot enter its states. Unassessed ranks remain inactive, have no initial disease or latent fraction, and contribute no canopy transport. Older observed disease retains visible damage while infectious tissue decays over the recorded assessment age. Conditional results do not validate the first field infection, sowing-to-onset simulation, leaf appearance or regional host dynamics. Initial-origin infections are not retrospectively assigned to primary or secondary sources.

Observation mappings
BASF P%INF is an unspecified visible infection percentage and remains a working visible-damage proxy. The damage proxy is nonsporulating plus infectious plus removed tissue, and excludes latent tissue. French pycnidial coverage maps to infectious plus removed tissue and excludes latent and nonsporulating stages; source senescence is not converted to zero. Laboratory incubation and pycnidial appearance supply separate process constraints. Source endpoints are not pooled as interchangeable severity measurements.

Evaluation contract
The model family and parameter bounds are frozen in analysis/primary_secondary/evaluation_contract.json before fitting. Forward2018and2019folds purge every test coordinate from earlier-year training. Those tests were inspected during the earlier baseline study and are reported as development-era transfer tests, not a newly untouched test set. Country-heldout tests keep coordinate-years and leaves together. Hyperparameter comparison uses grouped training-only inner folds. Independent French and newly acquired greenhouse datasets remain separately reported external tests. Any later design informed by an external result must label that dataset as development data and obtain a new final test.

Publication evidence
Required crop-protection evidence includes competitive prediction against strong same-information baselines, upper-leaf and lead-time evaluation, missed disease and false-warning assessment, uncertainty on independent field units, numerical invariants and convergence, and independent spatial and temporal transfer. Prospective weather and intervention outcomes are necessary for operational and economic claims. Quantitative primary–secondary attribution additionally requires source-identifying evidence; it is distinct from predictive validation of the combined epidemic model. Continental application requires regional wheat masks, sowing/phenology inputs and environmental extrapolation checks. Climate projections remain conditional and require supported historical application and documented scenarios. A complete manuscript contains actual supported results, inspectable figures, reproducible code/data and allowed verified references; absent evidence is not filled by invented results.

Current crop-protection diagnostics
analysis/primary_secondary/crop_protection contains the declared supplementary diagnostic contract, frozen-prediction analyses, lead-time and field-error figures, common-schema export and formal Methods/Results generator. The1%,5%,10%and25%cutoffs are diagnostic source-scale sensitivity checks, not validated spray thresholds. Observed weather after initialization is a retrospective forcing input, not a weather forecast. The model's compartment and initial-origin labels do not alter these claim limits.
